Engagement Party Invitation Wording

Engagement parties are the most relaxed event in the wedding calendar, and the wording can be too. The only rule that really matters: invite only people you plan to invite to the wedding.

What should an engagement party invitation include?

  • The couple’s names
  • The host (often parents, sometimes the couple or friends)
  • Date, time and place
  • RSVP details and dress code if it is not obvious

Formal wording

Mr. and Mrs. David Allen
request the pleasure of your company
at a dinner celebrating the engagement of their daughter
Claire Elizabeth
to
Thomas James Reed
Please join us in celebrating the engagement of
Claire Allen and Thomas Reed
Saturday, the sixth of March, at seven o’clock

Casual wording

She said yes! Join us for drinks to celebrate Claire and Tom.
Put a ring on it! Come celebrate the newly engaged couple.
Love is in the air — and on her finger. Engagement party for Claire and Tom.
Cheers to the happy couple! Backyard BBQ to celebrate Claire and Tom’s engagement.

Surprise engagement party

Shh! Tom is proposing on Saturday. Join us at 8 pm at The Garden Bar to surprise the happy couple.
Help us surprise Claire and Tom with an engagement celebration — please keep it secret!

Is gift wording okay on an engagement party invitation?

Generally no. Engagement gifts are optional, and many couples have not set up a registry yet. If you want to say it, keep it to “No gifts, please — your company is the perfect gift.”

When should engagement party invitations go out?

Three to four weeks before the party, which usually happens within two to three months of the proposal.

Wedding invitation wording

Wedding invitations follow a fixed order. Once you know it, the wording almost writes itself — here is the order, then examples for every hosting situation and style.

Quick answer

A wedding invitation lists, in order: the hosts, a request line (“request the honour of your presence” for a religious ceremony, “the pleasure of your company” otherwise), the couple’s names, the date and time, the venue and city, and a reception line. Keep registry details off the invitation.

What should the invitation include?

  • Host line — who is inviting
  • Request line
  • The couple’s full names
  • Date and time (spelled out on formal cards)
  • Venue name and city
  • Reception line, e.g. “Dinner and dancing to follow”

When the bride’s parents host

The most traditional format. The bride’s parents are named first and the groom’s full name follows “to”.

When both families host

Name both sets of parents, or use “together with their families” to keep it short.

When the couple hosts

Increasingly common, and perfectly correct at any level of formality.

Divorced, blended and late parents

Each divorced parent gets their own line, without “and” joining them. A late parent can be honoured with “the late”.

Reception-only and after-party cards

For guests invited to the celebration but not the ceremony. Make it explicit so nobody arrives early expecting vows.

Destination and weekend weddings

Give the date range, not just the ceremony date, and point to a website for travel details.

Faith-based wording

A short verse or blessing above the host line works for most traditions.

What should not go on a wedding invitation?

  • Registry details. Put them on your wedding website instead.
  • “No kids” or “adults only”. Show who is invited with the names on the envelope and a seat count on the RSVP card.
  • Abbreviations on formal cards like “St.” or “Sat.” — spell everything out.
  • “And guest” when you know the partner’s name. Use the name.

Frequently asked questions

When should wedding invitations be sent?

Six to eight weeks before the wedding, and ten to twelve weeks for destination weddings. Save the dates go out six to twelve months ahead.

Is it “honour” or “honor”?

“Honour” with a u is the traditional spelling on formal invitations, even in the US. “Honor” is also correct; just be consistent.

Whose name goes first on a wedding invitation?

Traditionally the bride’s name comes first when her parents host. For couples hosting or same-sex couples, use alphabetical order or whatever reads best aloud.

Should the year be written out?

On formal invitations, yes: “two thousand twenty-seven”. On casual invitations numerals are fine.

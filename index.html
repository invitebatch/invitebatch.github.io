How do you write a good invitation?

Every invitation answers the same five questions: who is celebrating, what the occasion is, when it starts and ends, where it is, and how to reply. Get those right and the tone is the fun part.

Lead with the person

“Join us for Maya’s 30th” says more than a paragraph of decoration.

Write the date in full

“Saturday, June 12, 2027” avoids 06/12 vs 12/06 confusion.

Add an end time

Guests plan babysitters, rides and the next party around it.

Make replying easy

One reply-by date and one clear way to answer.

Should the guest’s name be on the invitation?

If you can, yes. A card that opens with “Dear Aunt Rosa” feels personal, and it makes it clear exactly who is invited — which prevents most plus-one and children questions before they start.

For long guest lists, mail merge in Word, Canva’s bulk create, or a wedding invitation mail merge tool like InviteBatch can print a named card for every guest.

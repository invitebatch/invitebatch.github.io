How do you write a good invitation?

Every invitation, whatever the event, answers the same five questions: who is celebrating, what the occasion is, when it starts (and ideally ends), where it is, and how to reply. Get those right and the tone is the fun part.

  1. Lead with the occasion and the person. “Join us for Maya’s 30th” says more than a paragraph of decoration.
  2. Write the date in full. “Saturday, June 12, 2027” avoids the 06/12 vs 12/06 confusion for international guests.
  3. Give an end time for parties, so guests can plan babysitters and rides.
  4. Put RSVP details last, with a reply-by date and one clear way to answer.
  5. Read it out loud. If it sounds stiff when spoken, it will feel stiff on paper.

Should you put the guest’s name on the invitation?

If you can, yes. A card that opens with “Dear Aunt Rosa” feels personal, and it makes it clear exactly who is invited — which avoids most plus-one and children questions. For long guest lists, mail merge in Word, Canva’s bulk create or a batch invitation tool can print a named card for each guest.
